This is a real, competitive sport

Ultimate rewards the same things you're already good at: top-end speed, explosive cuts, vertical, and the will to sprint back on defense. If you play soccer, lacrosse, football, basketball, or track — or you're just a fast, competitive athlete looking for a spring/fall home — this is built for you.

Learn to Play is the on-ramp into that team. It's how brand-new players, especially incoming 9th-graders, get in — skills-first practice feeding into our two competitive programs: Tech Support (boys/Bx) and Disco Tech (girls/Gx & non-binary).

No experience needed to start. No cut, and nobody is turned away over cost.

You'll fit right in if you...

  • ✓Play (or played) soccer, lacrosse, football, basketball, or track
  • ✓Can run, cut, and jump — and want to compete
  • ✓Are new to Brooklyn Tech and looking for a team
  • ✓Have never touched a disc but are ready to work

What players actually build

  • ✓Conflict resolution — you talk through every foul and out-of-bounds call with the person who just guarded you
  • ✓Real-time problem solving — no coach can call a play mid-point; you read the field and adjust
  • ✓Accountability — you call your own fouls, which means your integrity is on display every point
  • ✓Respect for opponents — the traditional post-game spirit circle, win or lose

Ultimate is self-officiated. That's the point.

There are no referees. Players call their own fouls and lines, and settle disagreements with the person guarding them — in the middle of a competitive game, under real pressure. That's Spirit of the Game, the principle the entire sport is built on, and it's why college coaches and admissions officers take notice of it.

It's also why parents notice a difference: players who do this every week get better at negotiating, staying calm under pressure, and resolving conflict directly — skills that carry off the field.
